## 1. Data quality — the ≥0.90 tail is measured, not imputed

`scripts/check_trpb_imputation.py`.

871 of 160,000 TrpB fitness values are KNN-imputed and the CSV carries no flag, so every
K227/G228 conclusion rested on five unverified variants. The upstream flags are not
available offline, but the question is answerable from the landscape: KNN imputation
returns a *mean* of k neighbours, and a mean cannot exceed the maximum of what it averages.

At sklearn's default `n_neighbors=5`, **four of the five are ruled out outright**. The
survivor `CIKG` would additionally require the 76-way equidistant tie-break to have
selected exactly the five highest neighbours — 1 in 18,474,840. `AIKG` (the global
optimum) and `CLKG` exceed even the k=1 ceiling, so they are measured at any k.

**The K227/G228 finding does not rest on imputed values.**

## 2. Prior fitness-scale calibration — tried, measured worse, reverted

Bounding the prior to ±0.25 on the fitness scale was a regression:

| arm | best fitness | top-1% |
|---|---|---|
| calibrated (spread 0.25) | 0.7149 | 33.31 |
| raw magnitudes (default) | **0.7462** | **45.60** |
| paired diff | +0.0313 (t=4.77) | +12.29 (t=12.50), 46/48 seeds |

A 2×2 over {prior calibrated, raw} × {aux weights on, off} isolates it cleanly — the
calibration is the whole effect, with no interaction.

**Why the reasoning failed.** The scale argument was right about the statistics (a GP
prior *mean* has no business 15 units outside an observable range of −0.16 to 1.00) and
wrong about the consequence, because the term does not behave like a prior mean. It
behaves like a weighted ranking feature whose weight *is* its magnitude, and it carries
real signal — Spearman **+0.186** against measured fitness. Bounding it cut that weight
to near zero.

Checked against two mechanisms, both contradicted: not concentration (the calibrated arm
selected *more* distinct combos, 2746 vs 2241), and not catastrophic candidates returning
(the extreme tail is unchanged, 4.0% vs 4.2% of picks below raw −8). What changed was
broadly milder-quality picks — mean measured fitness of selections 0.1818 vs 0.2441.

`PRIOR_CALIBRATION_ENABLED` defaults **off**, kept as a knob. A sweep is monotonic in
allowed magnitude (33.31 → 36.12 → 38.29 → 41.21 → 44.88 → 45.60 raw), so there is no
small-spread optimum.

**Open:** `spread=8.0` gave the best *fitness* of any arm, 0.7564 vs raw's 0.7462, at
n=24 (~1.4 SEM). A 48-seed confirm would say whether a *tuned* bound beats unbounded.

## 3. Auxiliary Pareto objectives — keep them, but only in the rank

Synthesizability and developability entered selection multiplicatively,
`quality = acquisition × pareto × synth × dev`, with `dev` as low as 0.4.

Measured against TrpB fitness over 1,500 random variants:

| proxy | ρ vs fitness | p |
|---|---|---|
| synthesizability | **−0.1038** | 5.7e-05 |
| developability | −0.0286 | 0.27 (noise) |
| combined multiplier | **−0.0811** | 1.7e-03 |

On the five ≥0.90 variants the multiplier averages **0.7222 vs 0.7818** for random — a
7.6% relative penalty on precisely the candidates worth measuring. The two `V183C`
variants score 0.6432, so the proxies' cysteine aversion penalises two of the five best.

Removing the multipliers is **not separable** in either direction (best fitness t=0.24,
top-1% t=1.15), so the change is kept on parsimony. Weighting is now opt-in via
`apply_auxiliary_objective_weights` (default `False`); both scores are still computed and
reported.

**But the same proxies are valuable in the non-domination rank** — see §4. A candidate
only loses rank if dominated across all four objectives, which is a far gentler mechanism
than a 0.4× haircut. ρ = −0.08 against fitness means they are not fitness *predictors*,
which is not the same as being harmful in selection.

## 4. Component ablations — two components are better removed

> **CORRECTED 2026-10-31.** The table originally published in this section was
> confounded and is withdrawn. `PanoptesSelector` set
> `_mw.PRIOR_CALIBRATION_ENABLED = ablation not in ("raw-prior", "legacy")`, and
> `"none"` is also not in that tuple, so every `--ablate-components` arm ran with the
> prior calibration **forced on** while the baseline it was compared against had it
> **off**. Every delta carried the ≈ −12.8 top-1% calibration penalty from §2 in
> addition to the component effect. Fixed in `ddd9010`; re-measured below on identical
> seeds. **Two of the five original signs were wrong.**

`--ablate-components {dpp,gflownet,pareto,dkl,prior}`. Baseline is the shipped
default, 0.7462 best fitness / 45.60 top-1%, paired on 48 identical seeds.

| ablation | best fitness | t | top-1% | Δ | t | verdict | withdrawn claim |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **dpp off** | 0.7480 | +0.30 | **48.15** | **+2.54** | **+2.37** | **significantly BETTER** | −14.4 worse |
| **dkl off** | 0.7441 | −0.36 | **48.04** | **+2.44** | **+2.56** | **significantly BETTER** | −14.2 worse |
| gflownet off | 0.7444 | −0.25 | 44.85 | −0.75 | −0.86 | not separable | −10.7 worse |
| pareto off | 0.7503 | +0.70 | 40.56 | −5.04 | −6.98 | worse | −16.3 worse |
| prior off | 0.7172 | −4.13 | 32.81 | −12.79 | −8.49 | worse | −12.8 worse |

Of the five original claims: one was right, two were directionally right but
overstated by roughly 3×, and two had the **wrong sign**.

A consistency check that the correction is real: `prior off` reproduces **32.81**
exactly from the confounded run. It should, because ablating the prior makes the
calibration setting irrelevant — the confound only moved arms where the prior was
still live. That the one unaffected arm is unchanged, and the four affected arms all
moved, is what a genuine confound looks like rather than noise.

**Independently corroborated on a different seed block.** `docs/HANDOFF.md` §1 reports
45.21 top-1% for the default arm and **50.44 with `dkl,gflownet` jointly ablated**, on
held-out seeds 48–95. This table is seeds 0–47. Two different seed blocks, two
measurement paths, same direction: ablating `dkl` helps, and the joint removal helps
more than either single. That agreement is why the correction below is reported as a
result rather than as a repair.

**What now holds:**

- **Removing k-DPP batch diversity makes the engine better** (+2.54, t = +2.37). The
  tail of this landscape is one narrow motif — all five variants ≥0.90 carry
  `K227 + G228` — and a diversity objective spends budget spreading away from the only
  productive region. Registered as a cross-landscape prediction in
  `docs/preregistration-motif-narrowness.md`.
- **Removing the two-stage deep-feature GP also makes it better** (+2.44, t = +2.56).
  A plain Matérn GP is not merely adequate here, it is preferable. The withdrawn claim
  that this arm cost 14.2 hits was the basis for calling the surrogate "not the weak
  link"; that inference is withdrawn with it.
- **GFlowNet is not separable in either direction** (t = −0.86). Its contribution to
  selection is unmeasurable at n = 48, which is consistent with §7A's finding that it
  supplies no depth — the pool holds zero triples and quadruples with or without it.
  The earlier claim that it "does contribute" is withdrawn.
- **The Pareto rank earns its place** (−5.04, t = −6.98), and shows the
  reliability/frontier signature: removing it yields the *best* peak fitness of any arm
  (0.7503) and the worst hit count.
- **"Delete the prior" remains closed in the negative** (−12.79, t = −8.49). This is
  the one original conclusion that survives unchanged.

**The redundancy argument is withdrawn.** It read: the advantage over random is 44.5
hits while the five single removals cost 68.4 between them, ~154% of the total, so the
components must be redundant rather than additive. That 68.4 was the confounded sum.
The real total is ≈ **−13.7**, and two of the five terms are **positive**. So the
components are *sub*-additive, not super-additive, and the conclusion inverts: removing
any single component costs little or helps, yet the system beats random by 44.5 hits.

That relocates the open question. The advantage is not hiding in redundancy among
these five, because they do not account for it. It is more likely in something the
ablation set does not cover — the candidate generator, the EI acquisition itself, the
featurisation, or `_AnnotationDrivenPlanner`'s position restriction. **Widening the
ablation set should precede building the 2^5 joint design**, which would otherwise
spend 32 arms partitioning a small fraction of the effect.

## 5. Server — the budget cap was not a cap

`/api/cloudlab/dispatch` and `/api/campaign/ingest-curves` passed
`estimated_cost=request.nominal_cost_usd` straight into ledger authorisation, so a caller
priced its own spend. Sending `0.000001` authorised effectively unlimited physical
dispatches. Both now price through the Gatekeeper's `wetlab_assay` model and the field is
removed from both request models.

Real figures: **25 variants of a 397-residue protein costs $3,314.25**; 2 variants of a
238-residue one, $198.36. The previous defaults were $0.0005 and $0.0002 — understating an
audited round by roughly four orders of magnitude. **This is behaviour-affecting:** a
deployment with a small budget will now return 402 where it previously succeeded, and the
refusal states the computed price.

Also: `relax_structure_endpoint`, `cloudlab_dispatch_endpoint` and `ingest_curves_endpoint`
were `async def` with no `await` in their bodies, so FastAPI ran their synchronous SQLite
and CPU work on the event loop. Now plain `def`, handed to a threadpool.
`ingest_plate_endpoint` and `cloudlab_webhook_endpoint` genuinely await request parsing and
stay async; their post-await SQLite work is still on the loop and is **not** fixed.

## 7. Harness and analysis defects fixed

Each of these produced a result that looked valid and was not:

- **An arm completing 0 of N seeds reported `available`** with blank metrics,
  indistinguishable from a real null result. Five sweep arms failed on a bad keyword
  argument and all five reported that way. Now `FAILED`, with an error naming the count.
- **`compare_ablation_runs.py` silently dropped unrecognised round statuses.** The `dpp`
  arm logs 127 of 240 rounds as `short_batch` — a *completed* round with some proposals
  rejected — so the script took an early round as final and reported 14.44 top-1% for an
  arm that reached 30.06. Now skips only genuine failures and warns on anything unknown.
- **The `dpp` ablation gave itself a smaller budget.** Plain top-n let distinct notations
  collapse to one variant identity; the protocol rejects duplicates without backfilling, so
  the arm measured 120.04 variants against every other arm's 125. Deduplicated by variant
  identity; k-DPP's measured cost moved from −15.5 to −14.4 once the budget was fair.
- **Component ablations did not record their setting**, leaving five run folders
  distinguishable only by timestamp. Now written to the arm's notes.
