The loop

Six steps, in a fixed order, every round

The model proposes where to look. It never decides what to spend, what to run, or when to stop. Those are separate components, and you can see each one's decision in the ledger.

1

Ingest the target

UniProt annotations for your accession — active site, binding residues, domain boundaries.

rag_worker
2

Propose search boundaries

The language model reads those annotations and nominates residue clusters worth saturating. You confirm or overrule them before anything runs.

prompts → your review
3

Select the micro-batch

A Gaussian process over physicochemical deltas ranks candidates by expected improvement per dollar.

math_worker
4

Authorise the spend

The gatekeeper prices the batch cold or warm, then asks the ledger for the money. No authorisation, no run.

gatekeeper → ledger
5

Score on GPU

ESM-2 650M on an A10G, batched inside the container keep-alive window so you pay for tensors, not boots.

esm_worker
6

Record and re-fit

Scores append to an immutable evidence log. The surrogate re-fits and the next round starts narrower.

ledger → math_worker

What this does not show

Held-out Spearman on TrpB is −0.037. The surrogate's global ranking does not generalise across an epistatic active site from 125 measurements. Expected improvement still concentrates sampling in good regions, which is why the hit rate rises while the correlation does not. We report it because a lab deciding what to make deserves to know which claim is load-bearing: the enrichment is, the correlation is not.

How spending is capped

Every GPU call is priced before it runs and charged against your quota as an integer number of micro-dollars. A call that would exceed the quota is refused rather than trimmed, and the refusal is written to the ledger. When the quota is gone, campaigns stop; nothing queues up and nothing overdraws.
